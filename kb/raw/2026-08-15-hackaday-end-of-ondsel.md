## What Ondsel was

A commercial venture attempting to bring FreeCAD into professional markets, bridging the
gap between FreeCAD's capable but unwieldy interface and the polished UX of AutoCAD and
SolidWorks.

## Why it shut down

Ondsel failed to attract sufficient commercial backing. The article states the venture
"was not strong enough to attract such commercial partners," forcing the shutdown. The
company had taken venture capital but could not sustain operations.

## Contributions before closure

Aleksandr Prokudin, an Ondsel team member commenting on the article, notes "~150 pull
requests with new features, improvements, and fixes" from the Ondsel team were merged into
FreeCAD before the shutdown, largely landing in FreeCAD 1.0.

## The article's analysis

The piece draws a parallel to Red Hat-style open-source commercialization but concludes
"the business case was not strong enough," "ending for now the prospect of FreeCAD playing
in the big leagues" against proprietary competitors.

Prokudin pushes back: the realistic market is "smaller teams that are new ideas and aren't
married to specific software," not head-to-head enterprise competition.

## Community response themes

- **UX criticisms:** FreeCAD's inherited CATIA-influenced paradigm remains confusing
  despite Ondsel's improvements.
- **Stability:** multiple users report models breaking after minor adjustments.
- **Optimism about 1.0:** some see genuine progress toward competitiveness.
- One experienced CAD professional calls it an "unnecessarily obtuse piece of software."
